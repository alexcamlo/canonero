# Dune driving: what the game should model

Short notes for issue #20. Only facts that change the game design. Most driving advice comes from 4x4 guides and forums. Treat it as consistent field experience, not measured data.

For subsequent physics work, use [KDJ95 sand physics](kdj95-sand-physics.md) and its [vehicle reference](kdj95-reference.md). They qualify the momentum, softness, gearing and recovery assumptions below; this document preserves the rationale for the existing game mechanics, not validation of their realism.

## Where sand is soft or firm

- **Windward (stoss) face: firm.** Wind pushes grains up this side by creep and saltation ([Wikipedia: Dune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune)). Every guide says this side is the firmer surface and the one to climb. It is also the gentle side, about 10–15° ([practicalmotoring](https://practicalmotoring.com.au/?p=52963), [UW angle of repose handout](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~aloveles/ProjectGallery/AngleOfRepose.pdf)). You can recognise it by its wind ripples, the "wavy lines" ([carnity dune tutorial](https://carnity.com/forums/topic/14600-dune-crossing-tutorial-and-techniques)).
- **Slip (lee) face: soft.** Sand blown over the crest has not been compacted by the wind ([patrol4x4](https://www.patrol4x4.co.za/viewtopic.php?p=6884)). It builds up at the top and avalanches down at the angle of repose, about 32–34° ([Jericoacoara slip-face study](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/dynamics-of-sediment-storage-and-release-on-aeolian-dune-slip-fac/)). It looks smooth, with no ripple lines ([carnity](https://carnity.com/forums/topic/14600-dune-crossing-tutorial-and-techniques)). "The back side of dunes can be extremely soft."
- **Crests: loose and risky.** The fresh grains that are about to slip over a crest are loose. Guides describe "molasses-like dune tops" and warn that a slow approach stalls you at the crest, or leaves you hung up on it ([4wdingaustralia](https://www.4wdingaustralia.com/4x4/15-tips-for-sand-dune-driving/)). On contoured faces the *rounded* (convex) high points of the windward side are the firm line ([patrol4x4](https://www.patrol4x4.co.za/viewtopic.php?p=6886)). So a sharp crest is soft, and a broad convex shoulder is firm.
- **Hollows and bowls: soft. Wide corridors: mixed.** "Sand blown between ridges may not have compacted" ([4wdingaustralia](https://www.4wdingaustralia.com/4x4/15-tips-for-sand-dune-driving/)). Guides warn about "soft bowls" behind dunes. Interdune corridors that the wind sweeps clear can be firm, but some are soft too ([Wikipedia: Dune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune), [onX Coast Guard trail](https://www.onxmaps.com/offroad/trails/us/oregon/coast-guard-ohv-south-trail-1343-n2qvro195z95)). → Model *concave* ground as soft, and leave flat ground neutral.
- Heat makes sand softer, damp makes it firmer, and repeated wheelspin on one line softens it. None of this is modelled (no weather or moisture).

## What makes a 4x4 bog down

- **Losing momentum.** Every source says the same thing: keep moving and float on top. A slow approach is the classic way to get stuck ([Isuzu](https://www.isuzuute.com.au/discover/featured-stories/master-the-art-of-sand-driving), [CarAdvice](https://www.caradvice.com.au/717627/4x4-tips-sand-driving/)).
- **Wheelspin.** Power while stuck "will simply dig the wheels in deeper". Ease off, then reverse into your own compacted tracks ([carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/all-you-need-to-know-about-driving-your-4wd-on-sand-131010/), [BFGoodrich](https://bfgoodrich.in/auto/help-and-advice/faq-4wd-tips)). This is the game's existing rocking mechanic.
- **Tyre pressure.** Airing down is the number one factor. Sources give about 15–20 psi, compared with 30–40 on the road. A longer footprint floats better, but you should keep under about 25 km/h when aired down ([BFGoodrich](https://bfgoodrich.in/auto/help-and-advice/faq-4wd-tips), [GORV](https://emag.gorv.com.au/gorv-digital-magazine-issue-51/0381154001621912434/p38)).
- **Stopping on a slope or in soft sand.** "Stopping in soft sand or on any incline makes restarting much harder." If you have to stop, stop with the nose pointing downhill ([4wdingaustralia](https://www.4wdingaustralia.com/4x4/15-tips-for-sand-dune-driving/), [CarAdvice](https://www.caradvice.com.au/717627/4x4-tips-sand-driving/)). Uphill starts on soft sand are where you bog.
- **Weight and gearing.** Heavy vehicles need more pressure and still sink more. Low range keeps momentum at low speed without spinning up the wheels ([carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/all-you-need-to-know-about-driving-your-4wd-on-sand-131010/)). Front wheels should point straight when setting off, otherwise they plough and dig in.

## Braking hard going down a dune

- **Weight transfer digs the nose in.** On a slope, weight moves to the downhill axle ([tractor forum](https://www.orangetractortalks.com/forums/threads/keep-it-in-4wd.36774/post-306482)). Braking adds to that, so the front tyres push a mound of sand ahead of them and dig in ([carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/all-you-need-to-know-about-driving-your-4wd-on-sand-131010/)), while the light rear wants to come round. Braking is "just about the worst thing you can do" once a slide starts ([4wdingaustralia](https://www.4wdingaustralia.com/4x4/15-tips-for-sand-dune-driving/)).
- **Broaching.** If the tail comes round, the truck ends up sideways on a steep face. It is far easier to roll sideways than to tip over the bull bar ([4wdingaustralia](https://www.4wdingaustralia.com/4x4/15-tips-for-sand-dune-driving/)). "Sudden braking or extreme steering can lead to roll-overs" ([carnity](https://carnity.com/forums/topic/14600-dune-crossing-tutorial-and-techniques)).
- **Technique.** Point straight down the fall line in low range first, keep off the brake, and let engine braking and the sand hold the speed. If the tail starts to slide, a *touch of throttle* straightens it. Accelerate gently as the nose lifts at the bottom ([4wdingaustralia](https://www.4wdingaustralia.com/4x4/15-tips-for-sand-dune-driving/), [carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/all-you-need-to-know-about-driving-your-4wd-on-sand-131010/)). Never turn across a steep face. If you stall on a climb, reverse straight down.

## What this means for the game

1. Softness comes from the dune's shape. Lee faces (facing +x, downwind) are soft and windward faces are firm. Sharp convex crests and concave bowls are soft. Flat ground and broad shoulders are neutral or firm. Steeper ground (closer to the angle of repose) is softer.
2. Soft sand looks smooth and pale, and firm sand keeps its normal tone. Players can learn to read this, the same way drivers read ripples.
3. The existing sinkage model already covers momentum, wheelspin, rocking and airing down. Softness only has to scale how fast and how deep a wheel sinks.
4. Hard braking while pitched nose-down: the front wheels dig in (more sinkage and drag at the front), the rear goes light, and any yaw grows into a broach. Engine braking and a light brake stay safe.
